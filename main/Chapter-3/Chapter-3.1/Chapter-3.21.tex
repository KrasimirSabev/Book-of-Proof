\section{Lists}
A \emph{list} is an ordered sequence of objects, denoted by an opening parenthesis and followed by the objects, separated by commas: 
$$
(a,b,c,d,e)
$$
The objects are called \emph{entities}. If rearranged differently, then the lists are said to differ: 
$$
(a,b,c,d,e)\ne(b,c,d,a,e)
$$
Unlike sets, lists can have \emph{repeated entries}. This means that we cound the repeated entries towards the cardinality of the list. 

Two lists are said to be equal if they have \emph{exactly the same entries} at the \emph{exactly the same positions}. They practically behave like ordered pairs, so to speak. 

There is still an empty list, and it is denoted by 
$$
().
$$
